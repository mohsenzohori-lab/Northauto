Honda Odyssey vs Toyota Sienna: Used Minivan Guide

Choosing between a used Honda Odyssey and a used Toyota Sienna is the decision almost every Ontario family reaches once a sedan or SUV stops being big enough. Both are proven minivans with sliding doors, flexible seating and strong safety records, common on used car lots across Etobicoke and the Greater Toronto Area. This guide compares space, winter driving, reliability and running costs to help you pick the right one.

Two Proven Minivans With Two Different Personalities

The Honda Odyssey has been Honda's family hauler for decades, known for tidy handling (for a van), a clever interior and strong resale value across Ontario. Most used Odysseys in the GTA are 2015 to 2022 models running a 3.5-litre V6 and front-wheel drive. The Toyota Sienna built its name on durability and on one feature no rival minivan offered for years: available all-wheel drive. Used Siennas from 2015 to 2020 pair a 3.5-litre V6 with front-wheel drive or AWD; 2021-and-newer models are hybrids, again with AWD available. Neither van is a wrong answer — the right one depends on how your family actually drives.

Space, Seating and Cargo for Growing Ontario Families

Both vans seat seven or eight, swallow a full load of hockey bags, and use sliding doors that make tight Etobicoke parking lots and school drop-off lines less stressful than wide-swinging SUV doors.

Where the Odyssey pulls ahead

Families with young kids often love the Odyssey's Magic Slide second-row seats, fitted to 2018 and newer models. They slide sideways, making the third row easy to reach and keeping little siblings within arm's reach — or safely out of it. The cabin also feels a touch wider, and its third row folds flat into the floor in one quick motion.

Where the Sienna answers back

The Sienna counters with available eight-passenger seating, a deep cargo well behind the third row, and seats that stay comfortable on the long drive to the cottage. On either van, check that both sliding doors work smoothly under power, that every seat latch works, and that the rear entertainment system, if fitted, actually plays — power door repairs are a common used-minivan expense.

Winter Driving in the GTA: AWD Sienna vs Front-Wheel Drive Odyssey

This is the biggest real-world difference between the two. Every Odyssey is front-wheel drive. With a good set of winter tires, a front-wheel drive van handles most Greater Toronto Area winters without drama, but it cannot match an AWD Sienna when your Etobicoke street has not been ploughed or the cottage road turns to slush. If you regularly head north on Highway 400 in January, an AWD Sienna deserves a serious look. Two cautions: some AWD Siennas came with run-flat tires and no spare wheel, so check tire condition and cost, and remember that all-wheel drive does not help you stop — winter tires matter on either van.

Reliability: What to Inspect on a Used Odyssey or Sienna

Both vans have strong long-term reliability records, which is exactly why they hold their value in Ontario. Each has a few known watch-outs a pre-purchase inspection should cover:

  • Odyssey: the V6 uses a timing belt with a set replacement interval, so ask for proof it has been done. Check for transmission shudder at low speeds and any vibration from the engine's cylinder-deactivation system.
  • Sienna: the V6 uses a timing chain, so there is no belt service to budget for. Inspect the sliding-door cables, and on AWD models check the rear differential and transfer case for leaks.
  • Both: look underneath for rust. Ontario road salt is hard on brake lines, subframes and door sills, and a van rust-proofed every year will age far better.

A complete service history, a vehicle history report and an Ontario safety standards inspection will tell you more than any badge on the tailgate.

Fuel, Insurance and Everyday Running Costs in Ontario

The V6 versions use similar fuel in mixed GTA driving, while the 2021-and-newer hybrid Sienna uses noticeably less, especially in city traffic. Insurance quotes in Ontario vary more with your postal code and driving record than with which van you pick, so it is worth pricing both before you decide. Parts for either van are easy to find across the GTA and any competent independent shop can maintain them — one more reason both are low-risk used buys.

Our Take: Which Used Minivan Should Your Family Buy?

Choose the Toyota Sienna if all-wheel drive matters for your winters, or if you want the hybrid's fuel savings in a newer model. Choose the Honda Odyssey if you value the smarter second-row seating, sharper road manners and a cabin your kids will argue less in. Either way, buy on condition rather than kilometres alone: a well-maintained van with full records beats a lower-kilometre van with none, every time.
