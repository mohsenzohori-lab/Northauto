Bad Credit, New to Canada, First Car: How Used Car Financing Actually Works in Ontario

Your credit score is a starting point, not a verdict. Here is how lenders actually decide, what raises or lowers your rate, and how to set a payment you can comfortably keep.

How lenders look at your application

Lenders weigh four things: income stability (how long at your job), debt load (what you already owe versus what you earn), credit history (paid-as-agreed history helps more than the score alone), and the vehicle itself — newer, lower-kilometre cars are easier to finance than older ones. This is why a newer, lower-kilometre car can be an easier approval than a cheaper but older, higher-mileage one.

What changes your rate

  • Down payment: even $1,000–$2,000 down reduces the amount financed and can move you into a better rate tier.
  • Term length: longer terms (72–84 months) lower the payment but increase total interest; shorter terms do the opposite.
  • Vehicle age and kilometres: lenders cap terms by model year — ask us which cars qualify for the longest, cheapest terms.
  • Co-signer: a partner or family member with established credit can unlock bank rates.

New to Canada or first car?

Many lenders have newcomer programs that look at your employment and down payment rather than a Canadian credit file that does not exist yet. Bring your licence, an employment letter and recent pay stubs. A modest car at a payment you never miss is the fastest way to build the credit history that gets you bank rates on the next one.

G1 drivers — read this

Ontario lenders and insurers generally require a G2 or full G licence to finance and plate a car in your own name. If you are on a G1, the usual path is a licensed co-buyer or waiting until your G2 — talk to us before you fall in love with a car, and we will map out the quickest route.
