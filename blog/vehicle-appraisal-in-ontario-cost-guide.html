Vehicle Appraisal in Ontario: Cost & When You Need One

A vehicle appraisal puts a documented, defensible dollar value on a car — and in Ontario, more people need one than realize. Insurance companies, ServiceOntario, lawyers, and estates all ask for appraisals every day. For Etobicoke and Greater Toronto Area drivers, the good news is that a proper appraisal is quick and inexpensive: a flat $80 at a licensed dealer. This guide explains what a vehicle appraisal is, when you need one, what it costs, and what happens during the appointment.

What Is a Vehicle Appraisal?

A vehicle appraisal is a professional, written valuation of a car, truck, or SUV at a specific point in time. An appraiser inspects the vehicle in person — its condition, odometer reading, options, service history, and any damage — then researches what similar vehicles are actually selling for in the current Ontario market. The result is a signed document stating the vehicle’s fair market value and the reasoning behind it. Unlike a quick online estimate, a written appraisal is a document you can hand to an insurance company, a government office, or a court.

When Do You Need a Vehicle Appraisal in Ontario?

Insurance claims and agreed-value policies. After a collision or theft, insurers settle based on the vehicle’s value — and their number is not always fair. An independent appraisal documents what the car was really worth. Owners of classic, modified, or specialty vehicles also use appraisals to set an agreed insured value before anything happens.

Buying privately and retail sales tax. When you register a privately purchased vehicle in Ontario, tax is assessed on the purchase price or the vehicle’s market value, whichever is higher. If you bought a car below market because it needs work, a professional appraisal documenting its true condition and value can prevent you from being taxed on a number the car is not worth.

Family gift transfers. Transferring a vehicle to a spouse or eligible family member involves sworn statements and paperwork at ServiceOntario, and values sometimes need to be documented.

Estates and divorce. When assets are divided, vehicles need fair, documented values that both sides — and the court — can rely on.

Importing and donations. Vehicles brought into Ontario from the US or another province, and vehicles donated to charity for a tax receipt, both need an established fair market value.

What Happens During an Appraisal?

Most appraisals take about 30 to 45 minutes. The appraiser records the VIN and odometer, inspects the body, paint, tires, glass, and interior, notes options and modifications, and checks for accident repairs or mechanical issues that affect value. Then they compare your vehicle against current listings and recent sales of the same year, make, model, and trim in the Ontario market. To get the most accurate result, bring the vehicle’s ownership, your driver’s licence, and any service records or receipts you have — documented maintenance genuinely supports value. Be upfront about known issues, too: an appraisal that ignores damage will not stand up if the other side inspects the car.
