Honda CR-V vs Mazda CX-5: A Used SUV Buying Guide for GTA Drivers

If you are shopping for a used SUV in Etobicoke or anywhere in the Greater Toronto Area, two names come up again and again: the Honda CR-V and the Mazda CX-5. Both are compact crossovers with strong reliability records, reasonable fuel economy, and available all-wheel drive that handles Ontario winters well. But they drive differently, cost different amounts to run, and age differently as used cars. This guide compares the used Honda CR-V and the used Mazda CX-5 so Ontario car buyers can choose the right SUV for their budget, family, and daily commute.

Reliability and Long-Term Ownership Costs

The used Honda CR-V has one of the best reliability reputations of any compact SUV. Its naturally aspirated engines are proven over hundreds of thousands of kilometres, parts are affordable, and almost any mechanic in the Greater Toronto Area can service one. Maintenance costs for a used CR-V stay low, which is a big reason these SUVs hold their value so well on the Ontario used car market.

The used Mazda CX-5 is also reliable, though it sits half a step behind the CR-V in long-term durability rankings. Mazda's SkyActiv engines are well engineered and generally trouble-free, but some model years have had issues with infotainment glitches and suspension wear at higher mileage. Replacement parts for the CX-5 can cost a little more than Honda parts, so budget slightly higher for upkeep over the years.

Winner for reliability: the Honda CR-V, especially if you plan to keep the SUV for five or more years.

Fuel Economy: Used CR-V vs Used CX-5

Fuel costs matter to GTA commuters who sit in 401 traffic every day. On comparable model years, the Honda CR-V and Mazda CX-5 post very similar fuel economy numbers. Both front-wheel-drive versions are the most efficient; all-wheel-drive models use a bit more fuel but give you the winter traction Ontario drivers want.

As used cars, real-world fuel economy depends heavily on condition. Worn spark plugs, tired oxygen sensors, or underinflated tires can push either SUV well above its rated consumption. When you test drive a used CR-V or CX-5 in Etobicoke, ask for the recent service history and check that the tires are properly inflated.

Winner for fuel economy: roughly a tie, with a slight edge to the CR-V on most model years.

Winter Driving in Ontario: AWD and Handling

This is where the two SUVs split. The Mazda CX-5 has sharper steering and better body control, so it feels more confident on dry pavement and winding roads. Many drivers prefer it on a sunny day.

The Honda CR-V's all-wheel-drive system, on the other hand, is tuned for exactly the conditions Ontario winters deliver: snow, slush, and icy intersections. It engages quickly and works well with good winter tires. The CR-V also has slightly more ground clearance than the CX-5, which helps when plows leave windrows across side streets in Etobicoke and the GTA.

Winner for Ontario winters: the Honda CR-V with AWD and proper winter tires.

Cargo Space and Family Practicality

For families, cargo space and rear-seat room often decide the purchase. The Honda CR-V wins clearly here. Its boxy shape and clever rear-seat design create more usable cargo volume than the CX-5, and the flat load floor makes it easy to fit strollers, hockey bags, and Costco runs. Rear legroom is generous, and visibility out of the CR-V is excellent.

The Mazda CX-5 trades some of that practicality for style. The sloped roofline looks good but trims cargo height, and the cabin feels a bit more snug. Interior materials on the CX-5 tend to look and feel more upscale, which some buyers prefer.

Winner for family practicality: the Honda CR-V. Winner for interior feel: the Mazda CX-5.

What to Check Before Buying a Used CR-V or CX-5 in Ontario

Whichever SUV you lean toward, a careful inspection protects your money. Here is what Ontario used car buyers should check:

Accident History and Vehicle Reports

Always review a vehicle history report before you buy. Look for reported accidents, insurance claims, and whether the SUV was ever registered outside Ontario. A clean report with consistent Ontario registration and regular service records is the best sign of a well-kept used car.

Rust and Underbody Condition

Ontario road salt is hard on vehicles. Check the wheel wells, rocker panels, and underbody for rust. Surface rust on older suspensions is normal, but bubbling paint or soft spots on body panels are warning signs. Both the CR-V and the CX-5 hold up reasonably well, but a winter-driven example with no undercoating deserves a closer look.

Engine, Transmission, and Safety Certification

Listen for unusual noises on a cold start, feel for hesitation or harsh shifting during the test drive, and make sure every warning light behaves normally. In Ontario, a used vehicle sold by a dealer must come with a valid safety certificate. Ask to see the safety inspection paperwork and confirm what was repaired to pass it.

Which Used SUV Is Right for You?

Choose the used Honda CR-V if reliability, cargo space, winter traction, and low running costs are your top priorities. It is the safer, more practical pick for most GTA families and commuters.

Choose the used Mazda CX-5 if you value driving enjoyment, a nicer cabin, and distinctive styling, and you are comfortable with slightly higher maintenance costs. It is the more rewarding SUV to drive every day.
