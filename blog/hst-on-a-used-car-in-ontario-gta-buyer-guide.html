HST on a Used Car in Ontario: GTA Buyer Guide

If you are buying a used car in Ontario, HST can add more to your final price than any other line on the bill of sale. In Etobicoke and across the Greater Toronto Area, a used car purchase from a dealer is subject to 13 percent HST, and understanding HST on a used car in Ontario before you shop helps you compare all-in prices fairly, budget for plates and registration, and avoid surprises at signing.

How HST Works on a Used Car Purchase in Ontario

In Ontario, the Harmonized Sales Tax is 13 percent. When you buy a used car from a registered dealer, HST is charged on the selling price of the vehicle. That means a car listed at one price will cost noticeably more once tax, licensing and any agreed extras are added, so smart GTA buyers always ask for the all-in, out-the-door total before comparing two cars.

Keep the bill of sale itemized: vehicle price, trade-in value, taxable difference, HST, licence fees, and any warranty you chose should each appear on its own line.

Dealer Sales vs Private Sales: HST and Tax in Ontario

The tax treatment is different depending on who sells you the car. A dealer sale in Ontario charges 13 percent HST collected by the dealer at the time of purchase. A private sale does not charge HST at the curb; instead, Ontario charges retail sales tax when you register the vehicle at ServiceOntario, generally based on the purchase price or the vehicle's wholesale value, whichever is higher.

  • Dealer purchase: 13 percent HST on the taxable amount, shown on your bill of sale.
  • Private purchase: tax paid at registration, plus you arrange your own safety certification and Used Vehicle Information Package.
  • Curbsiders: unlicensed sellers posing as private sellers are illegal in Ontario — buying from a registered dealer gives you OMVIC protection.

Why all-in pricing matters for Ontario used car buyers

OMVIC requires Ontario dealers to advertise all-in prices: if a price is advertised, it must include HST and all fees except licensing and any optional products the buyer chooses. If an ad price and a bill of sale do not line up, ask for the difference in writing before you sign. All-in pricing makes it much easier for Etobicoke buyers to compare a car in the GTA fairly, without mental math on every listing.

How a Trade-In Reduces the HST You Pay in Ontario

A trade-in does more than lower the amount you finance — it lowers the tax itself. In Ontario, HST is charged on the difference between the price of the car you are buying and the value of your trade-in. For example, if you buy a used SUV and your trade-in is credited at a meaningful amount, you pay HST only on the remaining difference, not on the full selling price.

Bring your ownership and service records to get a realistic appraisal. Selling your old car privately might bring more gross dollars, but you lose the HST saving and handle the paperwork yourself.

What Else Is Taxable When You Buy a Used Car?

Buyers in the Greater Toronto Area are often surprised by which extras attract HST. In general:

  • Extended warranties and service plans you choose are usually taxable.
  • Accessories and protection products added to the deal are usually taxable.
  • Licence plates, permit and registration through ServiceOntario are separate government charges, not part of the vehicle price.
  • Financing interest is not HST — it is the cost of borrowing, shown separately in your loan disclosure.

Ask which items are optional before you sign. A good dealer will walk through every line and remove anything you did not agree to.

How to Budget for HST, Plates and Registration in the GTA

Build your budget around the total, not the listing price. Start with the vehicle price, subtract your trade-in, add 13 percent HST on the difference, then add licensing, your insurance start-up costs, and a small reserve for the first service, winter tires or other seasonal needs in Ontario. If you are financing, look at both the monthly payment and the total cost of borrowing over the full term.

Get an insurance quote before you buy, too — Etobicoke premiums vary widely, and a car that looks affordable on price alone can become tight once HST, insurance and fuel are added.

Questions to Ask About HST Before You Sign

Before you commit to any used car in Ontario, ask: Is this the all-in price including HST? How much HST am I paying after my trade-in? Which fees are licensing, and which are optional products? Can I see an itemized bill of sale? Clear answers to those four questions protect you better than haggling over a small discount while missing a large tax or fee surprise.
